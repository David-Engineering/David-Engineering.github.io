VEX V5 ROBOTICS · PUSH BACK

Team 22603A

For the VEX V5 Push Back season, Team 22603A earned Tournament Champion and Design Award honors. I designed the robot's mechanisms, created the CAD, and tested the system. I worked across the full design process—from developing the intake and conveyor system to identifying jams, modifying the initial conveyor flaps, and validating the revised mechanism. I also drove the robot in competition.

Full mechanism designCADTesting & iterationCompetition driver

MECHANICAL SYSTEMS

Three-stage intake and transfer system

The robot used a sequence of mechanisms to collect, move, and deliver game objects through the machine.

01
Non-static flex wheel intake

A compliant flex-wheel intake gathered game objects at the front of the robot. Its non-static arrangement allowed the intake to move and respond during contact.

COLLECTION
02
Flapped conveyor stage

The conveyor flaps pushed collected objects toward the final stage while funneling them into position. This guided transfer helped prevent jams as objects moved through the robot.

GUIDED TRANSFER · JAM PREVENTION
03
Dual rubber-band rollers

Two rubber-band rollers formed the final stage, gripping the objects and pulling them into the scoring area for a controlled feed.

GRIP · CONTROLLED FEED

System sequence: intake → flapped conveyor → dual rubber-band rollers → scoring area.

DESIGN DECISION / INTAKE

Why a non-static flex wheel intake?

The team chose a non-static flex wheel intake to make collection more adaptable to different object positions and angles while improving collection speed. The design also supported faster matchloading cycle times, helping the robot return to the game more efficiently.

ENGINEERING ITERATION / JAM PREVENTION

Problem → Design change → Result

01 / PROBLEM
Objects jammed in the conveyor

The original flap design could catch on blocks as they funneled upward, interrupting smooth object flow.

02 / CHANGE
Shortened the initial flaps

The team shortened the initial conveyor flaps so the conveyor could keep running while the blocks funneled upward.

03 / OUTCOME
Continuous conveyor operation

The shorter flaps let the conveyor run continuously and skip over blocks as they moved into position, addressing the jamming issue.

Engineering takeaway: A small geometry change improved how the conveyor interacted with game objects and supported more reliable operation.
